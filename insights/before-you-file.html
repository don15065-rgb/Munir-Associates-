LITIGATION / PERSPECTIVE

Before a suit is filed: five questions worth answering

The right claim in the wrong forum can lose time before the merits are reached.

PERSPECTIVE

Litigation

4 min read

← All perspectives

The right claim in the wrong forum can lose time before the merits are reached.

Start with the remedy

A grievance and a legal remedy are not always the same thing. Before drafting, define the order you actually need: a declaration, an injunction, payment, possession or another form of relief. That choice affects jurisdiction, evidence and the pleadings.

Identify the right forum

Questions of territorial, subject-matter and pecuniary jurisdiction should be addressed at the beginning. A specialist statute may direct a dispute away from an ordinary civil suit. Applicable law and current limits must be checked for the particular matter.

Build a chronology

Dates, notices, payments and communications often determine the shape of the case. Put the documents in order and distinguish what is known from what remains to be proved.

Check time and interim risk

Limitation periods, procedural deadlines and the need for urgent interim protection may change the order in which work must be done. A delay can matter even when the underlying claim has substance.

Price the dispute realistically

Litigation has financial and commercial costs. Compare the likely course of proceedings with settlement, negotiation and other legally available avenues. The best route is one chosen with its risks understood.

A note on legal information

This article is general information and not advice on a particular matter. Jurisdiction, procedure and relevant law should be verified against the facts and current legal position.
